AI Voice · in development

Talk to your aircraft. In your own words.

Benefits

Why it helps

  • Speak naturally: “how fast are we going?” or “put Perugia Approach on the standby”, with no fixed commands to memorise
  • Ask the aircraft: position, nearest airfield, altitude, engine, traffic and radio, answered from your own instruments through the RB-01
  • No invented numbers: the software computes distances and bearings, and checks every number in an answer against your instrument data
  • ATC calls for you: clearances for your callsign as a short list with the readback text; a new frequency is only a suggestion until you confirm
  • Ignores the chatter: passengers and calls for other aircraft are recognised and left alone
  • Offline and open: runs on board on a dedicated Raspberry Pi 5, no cloud or subscription, GPL-3.0 software and open-weight models

Command

Say it your way

Voice commands and what RB-08 does
“Computer, how hot is the oil?”reads oil temperature
“Computer, tune the radio to one two two decimal five”standby 122.500
“Computer, put Perugia Approach on the standby”standby by station name
“Computer, direct to Lima India Romeo Zulu”destination LIRZ
“Primary, show the engine page”engine page
“Computer, quanto carburante nel secondo serbatoio?”reads fuel tank 2
“What a beautiful view of the lake”nothing: not for the RB

ATC

Clearances in writing

ATC calls for callsign I-ABCD and the readback RB-08 shows
“India Charlie Delta, climb altitude five thousand feet, QNH one zero one three”“Climb 5000 feet, QNH 1013, India Charlie Delta”
“India Charlie Delta, turn left heading two seven zero, descend two thousand feet, squawk four three two one”“Turn left heading 270, descend 2000 feet, squawk 4321, India Charlie Delta”
“India Charlie Delta, contact Perugia Approach one two zero decimal seven”suggests standby 120.700, after you confirm
“India X-ray Yankee, report downwind runway zero one”another aircraft: no action

Supplementary information, not certified avionics. Speech recognition and language models make mistakes: check every value on the display or the approved instruments, and always listen to, read back and follow ATC yourself.
